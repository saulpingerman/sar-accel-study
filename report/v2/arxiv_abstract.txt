TPUs reach their rated throughput only on dense bfloat16 matrix products, whereas SAR backprojection consists of indexed reads. Whether spotlight image formation can be written in the TPU's idiom, and what it then costs against a GPU and a CPU, was measured on three Umbra collections at native size, formed on a TPU v5e, a TPU v6e, an Nvidia L4 and a 16-vCPU AMD instance by exact backprojection, factorized backprojection and polar format, with every image scored against a float64 exact backprojection, itself checked against the vendor's own image. The factorized algorithm runs in kernels written for each device (Pallas on the TPUs, CUDA on the L4, C++ with OpenMP on the CPU). A thousand Panama images at float32 accuracy cost $0.76 on the L4, $1.62 on the v5e and $1.89 on the v6e, and 4.3 times the L4's figure on the CPU. The v6e is the fastest device, 1.5 times faster than the L4 per image at 3.8 times the hourly price. Float16 throughout the L4 pipeline raises the image error by 0.9 dB and lowers the cost by 19%; the TPUs' native single-pass products add 12 dB of error, concentrated in phase errors beside bright returns that would be expected to matter for coherent products. Polar format costs $0.42 per thousand on the L4, 1.8 times less than factorized backprojection there, but on a TPU, where its final resampling is a scattered gather, it runs 47 (v5e) to 51 (v6e) times slower than on the L4 and its image carries a -32 dB residual whose source was not found.
