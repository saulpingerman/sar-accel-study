Three Umbra spotlight collections were imaged at the vendor's native size (8.5 to 12.2 thousand pixels on a side) on a Google Cloud TPU v5e, a TPU v6e, an Nvidia L4 and a 16-vCPU AMD instance, by exact backprojection, factorized backprojection expressed as matrix products, and polar format. Each image was compared with a float64 exact backprojection. At float32 accuracy a thousand Panama images cost $2.91 on the L4, $3.01 on the v5e and $3.22 on the v6e, and the order changes between scenes; the CPU costs 14 times the L4. The v6e is the fastest device, at 4.6 s per image. The L4's float16 products give the cheapest image within 1.1 dB of float32 accuracy ($2.36); the TPUs' single-pass bfloat16 products are about 12 dB worse. Exact backprojection at 8 times profile oversampling was no more accurate than the factorized image on two of three scenes. Polar format is 2 to 46 times faster but displaces scatterers by up to 7.6 m at the scene corners; its correction costs under a second on the L4 and 42 to 104 s on a TPU and leaves a -32 dB residual of unidentified origin.
