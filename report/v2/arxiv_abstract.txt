Three Umbra spotlight collections were imaged at the vendor's native size (8.5 to 12.2 thousand pixels on a side) on a Google Cloud TPU v5e, a TPU v6e, an Nvidia L4 and a 16-vCPU AMD instance, by exact backprojection, factorized backprojection and polar format, and each image was compared with a float64 exact backprojection. The factorized algorithm was first run as one JAX program on every device and then given kernels written for each accelerator from a stage profile, Pallas on the TPUs and CUDA on the L4, which made it 1.8 to 2.0 times faster on the TPUs and 3.9 times faster on the L4 without changing the images. With those kernels a thousand Panama images at float32 accuracy cost $0.76 on the L4, $1.79 on the v5e and $2.09 on the v6e, and the CPU costs 54 times the L4; the v6e is the fastest device, 1.4 times faster than the L4 per image at 3.8 times the hourly price. The L4's float16 tensor-core final stage gives up nothing visible and 12% of the cost; the TPUs' single-pass bfloat16 products are 12 dB worse and no longer cheaper. Exact backprojection at 8 times profile oversampling was no more accurate than the factorized image on two of three scenes. Polar format is the cheapest algorithm once its geometric distortion is corrected, but the correction runs well only on the GPU and leaves a -32 dB residual of unidentified origin.
