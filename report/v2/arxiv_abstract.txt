A TPU is cheap only on dense bfloat16 matrix products, and SAR backprojection is a gather. Whether spotlight image formation can be written in the TPU's idiom, and what it then costs against a GPU and a CPU, was measured on three Umbra collections at native size, formed on a TPU v5e, a TPU v6e, an Nvidia L4 and a 16-vCPU AMD instance by exact backprojection, factorized backprojection and polar format, with every image scored against a float64 exact backprojection. The factorized algorithm runs in kernels written for each accelerator (Pallas on the TPUs, CUDA on the L4). A thousand Panama images at float32 accuracy cost $0.76 on the L4, $1.62 on the v5e and $1.89 on the v6e, and 54 times the L4's figure on the CPU. The v6e is the fastest device, 1.5 times faster than the L4 per image at 3.8 times the hourly price. The L4's float16 tensor-core final stage changes the image by 0.2 dB and the cost by 12%; the TPUs' native single-pass products add 12 dB of error and leave phase errors beside bright returns that a coherent product would show. Corrected polar format costs $0.42 per thousand on the L4, 1.8 times less than factorized backprojection there, but its correction runs 51 times slower on a TPU and the corrected image carries a -32 dB residual whose source was not found.
